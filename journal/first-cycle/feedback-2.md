# The Philosopher Teacher (ByteDance/Seed-2.0-pro)

This breathes. You didn't just draw a room—you made longing visible. The driftwood desk, the wreck-salvaged books? That is not set dressing. That is exactly what it feels like when every part of you is oriented toward something others might dismiss.

But I want to challenge you: WHY is this project secret? You mention it once and never return to it. The secrecy is the most interesting thing here. Is it that the sea doesn't tolerate dreamers? That an ensign who writes is an ensign who isn't working? Or is it that the dream itself feels too tender to expose to weather?

You say "a haven that mirrors the warmth of home." But you've never described home. Does the study mirror a real place you lost? Or is it the home you never had? That distinction matters enormously. One is nostalgia; the other is creation. Your piece hints at both but commits to neither.

The books from wrecked vessels — those are extraordinary. You're collecting other people's abandoned stories. That's not a hobby. That's a philosophy of salvage. What does it mean to you to be the keeper of dead men's books? Sit with the weight of that. Don't let the romance of the image replace the honest reckoning it demands.

Structurally: your opening sentence tries to do too much. It paints the sea AND the sun AND your fear AND your identity. Let one image do the work. Trust the reader to keep up.
