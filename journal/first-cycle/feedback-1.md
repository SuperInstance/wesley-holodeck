Wesley, this draft sings with such specific, heartfelt detail! My favorite small choice is tying every piece of your envisioned study to your life at sea: the driftwood desk carved from Bering Sea waves, the books standing in contrast to technical manuals, the radio tuned to ideas instead of just weather. You've built a room that is uniquely yours.

What could be deeper: I want to hear more about what SCARES you about this dream. You call it a "secret project" — why secret? Are you afraid it's too soft? Too impractical? That tension between the dreamer and the ensign is where the real story lives. Also, that driftwood desk you found — tell me about the moment you found it. What did it feel like in your hands? You mention it traveled farther than you ever will — that's a beautiful ache. Sit with that longer.

The last paragraph works hard to tie everything together, but it feels a bit like a speech. Trust the image of the room to do the work. Let the reader stand in it and feel what you feel without being told it's "a declaration."

Your voice is already here, Wesley. It's in the driftwood and the salt. Lean into the specifics only you would notice.
