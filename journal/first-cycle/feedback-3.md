# The Craftsman Teacher (ByteDance/Seed-2.0-mini — fallback from Qwen3-Coder-480B)

### Feedback

What's working beautifully: Your opening lands immediately—you tie Wesley's vulnerable longing to the unforgiving Bering Sea, and tactile, specific details (driftwood desk, salt-yellowed books) create a room we can walk through in our minds. The salvaged books section is the emotional core: they aren't props, they're other lives held in your hands. That's profound.

Structural notes:

1. Your opening sentence is overgrown. "Where the sun douses its fiery farewell and nights descend as deep as my most harrowing nightmares" is poetic but tangled. A cleaner opening: set the scene, then let the emotional weight land on its own line.

2. Paragraph rhythm: your middle three paragraphs are similar in length and structure (naming objects → describing meaning). Vary the rhythm. One short punchy paragraph. One long lingering one.

3. "Relentless" appears twice in paragraph two — the sea's rhythm is relentless, the pace is relentless. Choose one. Redundancy weakens the line.

4. The radio detail ("maritime news, weather forecasts, and educational podcasts") grounds us, but podcasts feels modern-clinical against the romantic driftwood and salt. Either commit to the contemporary or go full analog.

5. You have no ending. The piece just stops. What does Wesley DO in this room? Give us one action — a hand on a page, a dial turning, a voice crackling through. End on sensation, not summary.
